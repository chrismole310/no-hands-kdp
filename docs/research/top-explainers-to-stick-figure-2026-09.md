## 3. What the top scripts actually do (from 10 transcripts)

Measured: 1,100 to 2,800 words for 7 to 18 minute videos, 140 to 195 words per minute. The stick-figure hits sit at the fast end (Ink Explainer ~225 wpm). Slower pace goes with cinematic footage that carries dead air. Stick figures cannot carry dead air, so write dense.

Opening moves, in order of frequency:
1. **Second-person present-tense scene.** "You're driving past a truck stop at midnight." "You pick up your phone, say hello, and all you get back is silence." "Imagine you are standing in front of a grill." "Right now, as you're watching this, there's a quiet hum somewhere in your house." Nine of ten openings put the viewer in a scene inside the first sentence.
2. **The obvious question nobody asks.** "Why was that meat from a cow and not from a lion?" "Why is that guy sleeping in a truck when there's a hotel across the street?"
3. **The pre-emptive rebuttal.** "Most people assume it is just a cultural thing. But that is only the surface." "It seems like a strange choice. But here's the thing, it's not a choice at all."
4. **The explicit promise with a time stamp.** "By the end of this video, you will understand exactly what calculus does." "By the time this video is over, you'll realize you've been watching one of the most precisely engineered systems in the world."
5. **Catalog videos skip all of the above** and open on item one immediately. "Sonder. You're sitting in traffic…" "O positive. O positive is the single most common blood type…" The list is the hook.

Body moves:
- Named tiers or zones ("sunlight zone, twilight zone, midnight zone"; "phase one, phase two"; "Precambrian… S-tier, F-tier"). Every hit segments the middle into labelled chunks the viewer can feel progressing.
- One concrete number per beat ("a lion keeps only 10% of the deer's energy", "3 seconds", "77,000 years").
- Escalation: each item is stranger, deadlier, or closer to the viewer than the last.

Closing moves:
- **Return to the opening image with new meaning.** Truck cab: "That's not someone who couldn't afford a room. That's someone 900 miles from home, legally required to be exactly where they are." Predator meat: "They made it feel wrong. And you are here today because it worked."
- **A one-line reframe of the viewer's own life**, never a summary.
- Catalog videos end on the most emotionally loaded item, not a recap.

Failure pattern: the two longest transcripts (41 min, 29 min) are the ones padded with sponsor reads and "if you learned something hit like." The 8 to 12 minute hits have no CTA until the final ten seconds, if at all.

## 4. The adaptation rule

Take the **format** and the **script mechanics**, never the specific video. Every script below is an original topic that sits in a proven format. Copying a top video's exact title and beat list is what the 80 ancient-humans clones are doing, and it is why they sit at 300 views.

The four-question test for a port:
1. Can every beat be drawn as one stick figure in one situation? (If it needs a landscape or a real face, no.)
2. Is the viewer the protagonist? (Second person, or a catalog where each item is "you".)
3. Is there a modern-inconvenience or private-habit angle? (This is what the Ink Explainer audience shares with the Brainosophy and PsychToons audiences.)
4. Can it be sourced from real papers or books listed in the description? (The 9.4M video cites DOIs. The clones do not.)
