# Stick-Figure Explainer Trend — Deep Research (28 Sep 2026)

Reference channel: **Ink Explainer** (@Inkexplainer96), the channel in the screenshot.
Data sources: Nexlev channel/video analytics (niche overview, similar-channel search, faceless outlier feed, batch RPM), live YouTube search, transcript + comments of the 9.4M-view video, Higgsfield workflow catalog, web search.

---

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

**Yes, there is still room, but not in the exact lane Ink Explainer owns.** The "stick figure + ancient humans" combo is the single most copied faceless format of summer 2026. Of 140 channels Nexlev ranks as most similar to Ink Explainer, **80 were created between April and September 2026** (10, 7, 16, 16, 15, 16 per month). A live YouTube search for "stick figure ancient humans" in the last 30 days returns dozens of brand-new "Stick___" channels sitting at 1–300 views. At the same time, sub-10K channels are *still* breaking out in August and September with 300K–940K-view videos, which means the format itself is not dead; the algorithm is still feeding it. The opportunity is to take the *visual language and script formula* into adjacent curiosity topics where the same audience lives and competition is 1/10th as dense.

## 3. What the winning script actually does (from the 9.4M transcript)

11 min 13 s, ~2,500 words (≈225 wpm narration), fully sourced description with DOIs.

Structure:
1. **0:00–0:15 Second-person present-day pain.** "This morning, a machine screamed at you until you woke up… your day was already decided."
2. **0:15–0:30 Hard cut to the past.** "Now, rewind 50,000 years. A man wakes up with no alarm…" Ends on the question the viewer "has never been free enough to ask".
3. **0:30–1:00 Pre-empt the objection.** "Your brain is already fighting this… ancient life was brutal… that was the official story. But the proof it's wrong was buried in the ground."
4. **1:00–3:00 Physical evidence.** Two skeletons compared. Concrete, visual, easy to draw.
5. **3:00–6:00 The named researcher with a stopwatch.** Richard Lee, Ju/'hoansi, "a number nobody believed".
6. **6:00–9:00 Artifacts and social life.** Beads, bone flute, "relationships are the bank account", biphasic sleep.
7. **9:00–11:13 Full-circle ending.** "The alarm that screamed at you this morning is just the latest payment." Ends on the same line as the hook.

Comment section (2.9K comments, 51K likes) is almost entirely emotional, not factual: "See everyone at work on Monday" (6.7K likes), "No taxes, no money, no politicians" (2.8K), "Civilization is a trap?". **The video sells a feeling about modern life and uses prehistory as the vehicle.** That is the replicable core, and it is independent of the ancient-humans topic.

## 5. Can we auto-produce this style? Yes, and the tooling is already in our stack

I could not find "Paper Empires" artifacts in this repo or in the Higgsfield workspace project list, so the assumption below is that it ran through Higgsfield's `faceless-video` workflow with the **Paper Diorama** preset. The same workflow has a **Stickman Cartoon** explainer preset (`237dd06c-3729-4895-9672-1c623c4266e0`) and a **Hand Drawn** preset, so the style swap is a preset id change, not a new pipeline.

### What Higgsfield's workflow locks
- Style key + character/prop roster: `seedream_v5_pro` (1k res, style reference from `resolve_explainer_preset`).
- Motion blocks: `minimax_h3`, one 10-second block per narration line, batches of up to 12.
- Narration: `text2speech_v2` (ElevenLabs variant), one locked voice, 7.8–9.5 s of speech per block.
- Captions: Whisper-timed, burned by the `subtitles` workflow.
- Assembly: `assemble_final.sh` in the Higgsfield sandbox; manifest is resumable.
- Also has a **Picture Story / still-pictures mode** (`assemble_slides.sh`): one held illustration per spoken beat, single continuous narration, Whisper timeline. **This is closer to what Ink Explainer actually looks like** (static ink drawings with light camera moves), and it is far cheaper per minute than motion blocks.

### The gap: video length
The workflow is designed around 30 s–2 min videos (N = duration/10 blocks, 12 per batch). Ink Explainer's money videos are 8–15 min. A 10-minute motion-block video is 60 minimax clips; a 10-minute still-picture video is ~80–100 images plus one narration. The API did not expose per-generation credit prices, so before committing I would run one 60-second test in each mode and read the credit delta (workspace currently holds ~3,276 credits on the Ultra plan).

### Proposed production line (per video)
1. **Topic engine** — Nexlev `faceless_outliers_videos` + `search_viral_videos_small_channels` weekly, filter outlier ≥ 5, subs ≤ 30K, format = animation. Pull title patterns, not topics.
2. **Script** — Claude writes to the 7-beat template in §3, target 2,300–2,600 words, must cite 6–10 real sources (DOI or book) in the description; the audience and YouTube both reward this. Validate with Higgsfield's `validate_motion_script.py` or `validate_picture_story.py`.
3. **Assets** — one style key + 8–12 recurring characters/props via Seedream with the Stickman (or Hand Drawn) preset as reference. Reuse across the whole channel; that is what gives the channel a "look".
4. **Visuals** — still-picture mode by default (1 image per ~7 s beat). Promote only the hook and the ending to minimax motion blocks. That gets the pacing viewers expect without 60 video generations.
5. **Voice + captions** — locked voice pair, single narration take, Whisper captions.
6. **Thumbnail** — Higgsfield `thumbnail-generation` workflow. The winning pattern is 2–3 words of big text on the drawing ("RAINED ALL WEEK", "WHY US?", "TRAVEL HOW?") plus a stick figure in an emotional pose. Nexlev `generate_thumbnail` is a second option.
7. **QA gate** — Nexlev `virality_predictor` on the cut, then upload.

Realistic cadence: 3 videos a week per channel once the asset roster exists.

## 6. Topic ideas that are eye-grabbing (grouped by lane)

Title formulas that worked across every breakout in the dataset:
- *"What did ___ do when/for/before ___?"*
- *"How did ___ survive without ___?"*
- *"When did humans start ___?"*
- *"Why are we the only ___?"* / *"Why don't we ___?"*
- *"Do animals know ___?"*
- *"The darkest / disturbing / craziest ways ___"*
- *"Psychology of people who ___"*
- *"What happens to your body when ___"*

The common denominator is a **modern inconvenience or private habit projected onto a world that did not have it**, delivered in second person.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Don't clone Ink Explainer's channel.** Build one channel on the *style + script formula* with a broader curiosity promise ("how humans actually work"), so it can move between lanes A–E without retraining the audience. Ink's own channel description already does this; its execution just happens to be stuck on prehistory.
2. **Launch order:** 2 ancient-humans videos from list A for the initial algorithm push (fastest proven traffic), then alternate psychology and animal topics, then money. Watch which lane's CTR and AVD hold up and double down.
3. **Pilot the pipeline this week:** one 60-second still-picture test and one 60-second motion-block test in the Stickman preset, read the credit delta, then decide the per-video budget for 10-minute cuts.
4. **Plan the back-end now.** RPM is $2–4, so a 100K-sub channel here is a $2–5K/month AdSense asset. The real money is the course/ebook upsell Mack is already running, which is also where this repo's KDP automation fits: every strong video is a chapter.
